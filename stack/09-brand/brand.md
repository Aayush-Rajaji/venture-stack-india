### Is the name defensible?

`[TBD]`

Check now, while changing it is cheap:

- **Is the `.com` (and your local domain) available or owned?**
- **Does anyone else in any city trade under a similar name?** Search properly — a similar business under a similar name three cities away will cause confusion and eventual conflict.
- **Is it registrable as a trademark?** Generic and descriptive names are hard to protect. "Prime Leather" or "AB Services" cannot really be defended; a distinctive coined name can.
- **Does it survive being said aloud on a phone call?**

> **The moment to fix a name is now, not later.** Early it costs a weekend and some printing. Two years in it costs your search ranking, your reviews, your customer recognition, and every piece of collateral you own — and by then someone else may have filed the mark you needed.
>
> If you decide to keep a weak name, that is a legitimate choice — record the reasoning and the risk you have accepted, and file what protection you can.

## Design system

Everything the AI needs to produce something on-brand without being told each time.

### Do not invent this — sample it

If you have a website, your palette, typeface and logo are already specified there to the hex digit. Read the values off the live site rather than eyeballing "our blue." A near-miss palette reads as a template with your name on it; the exact values read as *yours*, before a single word lands.

[`site2deck`](https://github.com/Aayush-Rajaji/site2deck) automates this: it samples colours, fonts, logo and icon set from a live site and turns them into a token file that produces branded, offline, single-file slide decks. This section is written to hand off to it cleanly.

### Rules

House style, so output is consistent without re-specifying it every time. Adjust to taste; the point is that a decision exists.

- No emoji in any produced document, deck or interface. Text labels and colour carry status. (The ☐ ✓ ✗ glyphs in these worksheet templates are structural markers, not emoji, and are exempt.)
- Depth comes from hairline borders and one dark register — not from drop shadows or gradients.
- No gradients on type. No pill-shaped call-to-action buttons.
- Two type registers used consistently: display for headings, tracked-uppercase labels for structure.
- Hierarchy comes from role, not from improvised font sizes.
- Any unverified number in a draft is visibly marked so it cannot pass for final.

**Photography:** real product, real work, real people. Founders under-invest here and then wonder why their deck looks generic. The photograph of your technician in uniform doing the job is worth more than any stock image, and it costs a phone call.

Compress before use — around 1600px on the long edge at moderate quality. It is the difference between a 4 MB attachment and a 15 MB one that nobody opens.
