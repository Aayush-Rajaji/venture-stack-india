# The List of 100

> A list of five prospects can easily produce zero. A list of a hundred, worked properly, reliably produces a handful.
>
> This is the single most common gap between founders who get traction and founders who conclude their channel does not work. The instruction is not "improve your pitch." It is: **make the list a hundred, work it through, and come back with what you learned.**

One list per channel. Build it in a spreadsheet; keep this file as the definition of the channel and the cadence.

## Building the list

Where the names come from. Use several sources — one source gives you one kind of prospect and you will hit the end of it at forty.

| Source | Names found |

| Maps / local search, systematically by area | |

| Alumni network, university offices, professional bodies | |
| Existing customers' referrals | |

| Walking a neighbourhood and writing down every one you pass | |
| Social platform search on the right job title | |
| **Total** | |

> **Ask for introductions explicitly.** Alumni offices, incubators, chambers of commerce and your own customers will make introductions if you ask for something specific and small. "Could you give me two or three names of offices here I could approach?" works. "Do you know anyone who might be interested?" does not.
>
> A small gift and a business card go a long way when you are the one asking for a favour. One product, nicely presented — not your whole range.

## The cadence

Applied to every name. Most deals are lost to silence, not rejection — and founders stop at two touches.

| Step | When | What | Done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Day 0 | Email or message: who you are, what it is, the order sheet or a link. Make saying yes trivially easy. | |
| 2 | Day 7 | Follow-up. A different angle, not a repeat of the first. | |
| 3 | Day 14 | Second follow-up. | |
| 4 | Day 21 | Phone call. | |
| 5 | Day 28 | **Visit in person.** | |

**Daily target:** `[e.g. 10 outbound touches every working day]`

Ten a day is 200 a month. That is the whole difference.

## The in-person pitch

For channels you can walk into. Thirty seconds, then a specific ask.

> *"Hi, I'm `[name]`, I run `[company]` — we're based right here in `[area]`. We make `[what]` for `[who]`. I'm trying to test it in the local market. Could you keep five pieces on your counter for two weeks? I'll leave them and a flyer, and I'll come back to collect your feedback either way. If they sell, here's the order sheet."*

What makes it work:

- **Risk removed.** Free or on consignment, small quantity, short window, you come back regardless.
- **Local.** "I live in this neighbourhood" and "please support a local business" both work, and they are true.
- **One flyer, one order sheet.** They cannot stock you if they do not know their margin, your minimum order and how fast you deliver. See [`trade-order-sheet.md`](trade-order-sheet.md).
- **Easy reordering.** A QR code that opens a WhatsApp message with the order already drafted.
- **You are also buying information.** Ask what they think will sell, what price they would put on it, what they already stock in that category.
