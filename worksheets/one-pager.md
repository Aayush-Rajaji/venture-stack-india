# One-Pager

The single most reusable document you will make. One side of A4, sent ahead of a meeting, attached to a cold email, handed to a partner, forwarded by an introducer to someone you will never meet.

Three variants, same skeleton. Build the investor one first; the others are edits of it.

---

## Institutional / B2B one-pager

Same skeleton, four changes. Buyers ask different questions than investors.

- **Drop** the ask and the market size. They do not care.
- **Add specifications and certifications.** Institutional buyers need the paperwork more than the story.
- **Add proof:** existing deployments, references, warranty and service terms.
- **Lead with the outcome for them,** not the size of your opportunity.

## Partner / channel one-pager

- **Lead with what they earn.** Margin, volume, exclusivity if any.
- **Show the support:** collateral, training, delivery times, returns policy.
- **Remove everything about your fundraising.** It reads as instability to a channel partner.

## Rules

**One page.** If it runs to two, one of them will not be read, and you do not get to choose which.

**Every number sourced.** Assume the reader checks one, and assume it is the one you were least sure about. Anything you have not verified is marked as an estimate or left out.

**Say the stage honestly.** "Prototype, in pilot with two customers" is a fine thing to be. Describing a prototype as a platform is the kind of thing that gets discovered in the second meeting and costs you the third.

**Date it and version it.** These get forwarded. A year later someone will quote a number back at you and you need to know which document they are holding.

**No stock imagery.** A photograph of your actual product, your actual team, or your actual work. Nothing else.

**Same numbers as everywhere else.** Deck, website, plan, stack. Check before sending. Inconsistency across your own documents is found by the one person doing arithmetic and costs more than the number was ever worth.

**Readable at a glance.** Generous type, clear headings, white space. A reader gives this thirty seconds on a phone. Dense text loses the reading before the content gets a chance.

## The eight-word line

The hardest part of the page, and worth an hour on its own.

> ✗ "Revolutionising the home services experience through a technology-first platform"
> ✓ "App-based appliance repair and annual maintenance contracts"

> ✗ "Democratising access to sustainable materials for the fashion industry"
> ✓ "Leather alternative made from crop waste, sold to manufacturers"

Name the customer, the thing, and the mechanism. Cut every adjective. If a stranger cannot repeat it back after hearing it once, it is not finished.
