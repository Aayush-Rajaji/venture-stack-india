Stack Section 02

Customer — Problem, Job to be Done, Evidence

Who the customer is as a person, the job they are hiring you for, what they do today instead, the interview evidence behind those claims, and the ICP used for targeting.

Customer

The section that everything else depends on and that is most often filled in from imagination. The test for this file is simple: how many of these claims came from a conversation with a real person, and how many came from you thinking hard? Both are allowed. Only one of them may be tagged source-of-truth.

If you have more than one customer type, write each one separately and mark which is primary. The most expensive early mistake is trying to serve three customers with one message.

Include "doing nothing" as a row. It is usually your largest competitor and it never gets named in a competitive analysis.

How often, and how much it hurts

Frequency[TBD — daily / weekly / monthly / a few times a year]

A problem that hurts badly but once a year is a hard business. A mild annoyance that recurs daily can be a great one. Know which you have — it determines your entire pricing and retention model.

Willingness to pay

What do they pay today for the alternative?[TBD]
Who holds the budget?[TBD]
What price have you actually tested?[TBD]
Has anyone paid you money?[TBD — how many, how much, when]

Stated willingness to pay is close to worthless. "I would definitely buy that" is what people say to be nice to a founder standing in front of them. The only evidence that counts is money that has moved, or a signed commitment to move it.

Every customer is a line in a sheet: date, what they bought, satisfaction, what they said, whether they came back. Not a fact somebody on the team half-remembers. This costs nothing to start on day one and is impossible to reconstruct on day four hundred.
