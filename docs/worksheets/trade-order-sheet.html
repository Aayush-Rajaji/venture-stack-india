Trade / Order Sheet

Trade / Order Sheet

You need two documents and most founders have neither.

>

A customer flyer sells the product to the person who will use it — visual, benefit-led, A5, glossy.

A trade sheet lets a shop, distributor or partner say yes — price, their margin, minimum order, delivery time, how to reorder.

>

A shopkeeper cannot stock you if they do not know their margin. This one page converts better than any conversation they have to remember.

This is the trade sheet. Keep it to a single side.

Removing the risk entirely is what gets you shelf space. You are buying information as much as a sale.

---

What has to be on it, and why

ElementWhy it matters
Their margin, stated as a numberThe only figure they actually care about. Do not make them calculate it.
Minimum orderWithout it they do not know if they can afford to try you
Delivery time, per productDifferent products, different lead times. Say so — a missed delivery costs the relationship.
How to reorder, in one actionA QR opening a pre-drafted WhatsApp message beats a phone number they have to find
BarcodeAny organised retailer needs one. Get them early — it is cheap and it blocks you at exactly the wrong moment.
A photograph of the productThey are choosing shelf space; they need to see what will sit on it
Your name and faceTrade is a relationship business. They should know who to call.
A date on itPrices change. An undated sheet gets quoted back at you a year later.

Print

  • A4 or A5, one side. Two sides gets lost.
  • Glossy if you can afford it, matte if you cannot. Do not print at home.
  • Take fifty with you every time you go out. You will always run out sooner than you expect.
  • Print in your customer's language, and in a local market test the local language against English rather than assuming.

The customer flyer is a different document

Do not merge them. Trade prices in a customer's hands undercuts every retailer stocking you, and a shopkeeper handed a consumer flyer still cannot work out their margin.
